Hospitality feels personal when the next suggestion reflects what the guest actually wants. Useful context helps a team recognize that preference and respond with care.

### Listen without assuming
An anniversary, an early flight or a need for quiet can shape a recommendation. Ask for missing details rather than treating the occasion as a budget, a purchase or a personal profile.
Offer choices the hotel can provide. Explain price and availability, and let the guest decide how far to continue.

### Support the person delivering the stay
Carry the relevant details to the team responsible for the next action. An employee should be able to understand the request, review its status and take over when judgment is needed.
For a returning guest, confirm identity and permission before reusing saved preferences. Current-stay context and longer-term memory serve different purposes.

### Judge the experience as well as the sale
Review whether the recommendation helped, whether the request was handled and whether the guest needed to repeat themselves. Pair completed commercial outcomes with service feedback.
A useful first pilot makes both sides visible: what became easier for the guest and what became clearer for the team.

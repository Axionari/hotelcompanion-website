Sales reports show what was purchased. Guest conversations can add context about what people asked for, could not find or chose to decline. Used carefully, those signals help managers ask better questions.

### Separate evidence from interpretation
A guest who asks for a quieter room has expressed a preference. They have not necessarily agreed to an upgrade or explained why they declined another room.
Keep the guest’s words separate from inferred reasons. Connect an enquiry with its recorded outcome when identity, permissions and source records support the link.

### Look for useful patterns
Repeated questions may point to unclear arrival information. Requests for an unavailable service may suggest a demand worth reviewing. Neither observation is a guarantee that a new offer will sell.
Assign an owner to investigate the pattern. Check availability, costs, service capacity and guest feedback before proposing a change.

### Review the result
Introduce an approved change with a defined measure. Compare the outcome with an appropriate baseline and record what remains uncertain.
Agree access, retention and permitted use of conversation data before collecting it. Leaders need enough context to improve the service while respecting the guest’s choices.

A compelling demonstration is a useful start. A buying decision also needs a clear account of the work the product can support in your hotel.

### Choose one important journey
Define the guest’s goal, the people involved and the outcome that would matter. A direct-booking enquiry, transfer request or room-service order has different requirements.
Ask to see the complete journey, including manual operation, a change of plan and a handoff to a person.

### Confirm the operating fit
Review the knowledge required, supported connections, permissions and source of confirmation. Identify what works in the demonstration and what must be configured or validated for your property.
Name the owner of implementation, daily service and exceptions. Agree what your team provides and what the supplier handles.

### Make the purchase defensible
Set a baseline, eligible outcomes and review dates before the pilot. Measure completed business and contribution after costs alongside service quality and adoption.
A decision to expand should follow evidence that the journey works for guests, staff and the hotel’s economics. A clear stop or adjustment decision is useful evidence too.
